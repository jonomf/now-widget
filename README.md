# Now

An Android widget and lock screen notification for saying what you're doing right now, with one tap. There are no timers: each tap ends whatever came before it.

Install from [the latest release](https://github.com/jonomf/now-widget/releases/latest).

- Tap a different activity to switch to it.
- Tap the wrong one? Tap the right one within a minute and the mistake is replaced.
- Tap the current activity again to turn it off. The widget glows softly while nothing is on. A setting can make this tap move the start back instead.
- Larger widgets and the expanded notification let you mark who you're with; the biggest size and the expand panel also show places.
- The 📝 button is a check-in: a mood face, feeling words, where you are, a context (like a trip), and a note (use the keyboard's mic to dictate). Any one of them alone is fine, and it works while nothing is on.
- Diary days run from 4am to 4am, for today's totals, stats and the timeline.
- The timeline shows notes and feelings under the activity they were noted on; everything is editable.
- Import a Daylio backup (`.daylio`, Android or iOS) or Daylio CSV: you choose where each Daylio activity goes (a feeling, a Now activity, a person or a place). Each check-in becomes one feeling at its time. Importing the same or an overlapping backup again adds nothing. Export feelings back out as Daylio CSV.

- **Obsidian:** pick your vault folder once (Android's folder picker, no permission needed) and the app writes one note per diary day, `YYYY-M-D (now).md`, in the vault root, in your own log style (`#log-now`, an `At [[place]] w [[people]] in [[trip]]` line, one bullet per stretch, notes and feelings under it, a totals list). It only writes those notes, only when a day's text changed (about a minute after you change something, and when the app opens), and never reads them back. The vault's root notes are indexed (read-only, hourly and on "Rescan vault notes") so people, places and trips autocomplete from your notes and their aliases; names with no note just become plain links, and no note is ever created for them. A link that is one note's alias is written as `[[Note Title|Link title]]`, and a note renamed in Obsidian renames its link. A day stays marked as changed until its note was fully written, so a write cut short is redone. Add the line shown in Settings to your `_templates/index template` yourself. If Obsidian keeps the vault where the picker can't see it, move it to Documents or pick its parent folder.

- **Calendar:** after you allow calendar access (the app explains first), pick a log calendar (make one called Log on calendar.google.com and sync it on the phone) and map each activity group to one of your calendars, its area. With the export on, each finished stretch of 5 minutes or more (back-to-back stretches of the same activity and links merged) becomes an event like `🎨 Hobby · Sam, Dulce @ Tartine`, with that stretch's notes and check-ins in the description and a last line `now-log:<key>`. It goes into the log calendar in the Google event color nearest its area's calendar color, or into a log calendar per area (a setting). Google's own sync uploads it. Edits in the app update the event; nothing is read back. The app only ever changes or deletes an event it wrote itself: it must have its own record of the event, the event must still carry its `now-log` line, and it must be in a log calendar. Change the log calendar and old events stay put until you tap "Remove log events from …". An event you edit in Google Calendar (its `now-log` line changed) is let go: never updated, deleted or replaced again. Deleting a log event in Google Calendar doesn't stick, since the phone's log is the source; delete the entry in Now's timeline instead. Calendars are remembered by account and Google's calendar id, so if a picked calendar disappears or is swapped, nothing is written until you pick it again; an unsynced log calendar stops writing too. "Test calendar" writes one 5-minute test event and reports what the phone did with it. The timeline's "Planned" toggle shows the day's planned events from your area calendars against what you did, per area, with a match figure; while a planned event is on, its activity (by your keyword rules, or the area's most used activity) moves to the first widget button. Log events include notes and moods: anyone you share the log calendar with can read them.

- **Hub (sync with your PC):** the phone and the Now widget on your Windows PC ([jonomf/windows-widgets](https://github.com/jonomf/windows-widgets)) share one log through the **Now hub**, a Google Sheet in your own account with a small Apps Script web app (`hub/Code.gs` has the one-time setup; the format is `hub/PROTOCOL.md`). Both devices are equal: each keeps a full copy and works offline, every row is a record with a global id and a version, and for each id the newest version wins, so both end up with the same log. Paste the web app URL and key in Settings → Hub (checked with the hub; the key has its own preferences file, excluded from backups and transfers) and turn syncing on: the whole log is sent once (activities, people and places, moods, feeling words, entries, notes and feelings), then whatever changes, a few seconds after each change, when you open the app, and every 15 minutes, taking in the PC's taps, edits and notes at the same time. **When it's on, your log is in that sheet; anyone you share the sheet with can read it.** Activities, people and places are made on the phone; the PC's versions of those aren't taken in yet. The PC's entries come in like your own taps and are written to Obsidian and the calendar like everything else.
- **Toggl:** paste your Toggl API token (Profile settings → API token; checked with Toggl, kept in the app's private storage and shown only as `••••` and its last 4 characters), choose a workspace, and turn sending on. **This data goes to Toggl's servers.** Each finished stretch of a minute or more (back-to-back stretches of the same activity and links merged; the running one waits until it finishes; idle is skipped) becomes one time entry: project = the activity (made once, when first needed; on a plan without projects the entry has none and the activity still starts the description), description = `🎨 Hobby — notes joined with / ` (check-in moods and feelings only if you turn that on; notes on by default; kept under 2800 characters), tags = people, place and trip plus `now` on every entry. "Send past days" sets how far back to go (from the day you turn it on, 7 days, 30 days, or everything). Toggl's free plan allows about 30 requests an hour, so the app sends at most 25 an hour, oldest days first, one request about every 1.1 seconds, stops when Toggl's quota headers say it is nearly used up (or on HTTP 402/429), and tries again when the limit resets (or backs off, up to an hour, after errors); a long history trickles out in the background while the phone is online, and the settings show the last send, entries sent, days waiting, requests left this hour, the next attempt and the last problem. The app only ever updates or deletes entries it made itself, by the ids it recorded; it never lists or touches your other entries. Edits you make in Toggl aren't read back, and an entry you delete in Toggl is never made again. If an answer from Toggl is lost after a request, the next run lists what changed in Toggl since then (one request) and adopts an entry only if it has the same workspace, start, length and description and the `now` tag (and isn't deleted); if two entries match, it adopts neither, makes no second entry, and the settings say which day to check. An adopted entry is only recorded that run and acted on in the next. Days go today first, then yesterday, then the backlog oldest first; a run stops after 8 minutes and carries on later. With no connection it waits 2 minutes and tries again when a network is back. The token is kept in its own preferences file, excluded from backups and device transfers, together with the Toggl account it belongs to: a token for a different account asks whether to forget or set aside the entries sent to the old one. Moving "Send past days" later keeps already-sent entries updated and deleted as before; it just sends nothing new before the date. "Remove sent entries from Toggl" turns sending off and deletes only the recorded entries, within the hourly limit, over time.

The app's only internet use is the Toggl export (below), and only once you turn it on: the INTERNET permission is there for nothing else, and the app has no updater (the Widget Updater app does that). The log stays on the phone unless you turn on the calendar export, which hands events to Google's sync, or the Toggl export, which sends it to Toggl's servers. Moods, feelings and notes never show on the lock screen. Export to CSV from the app.

## Development

- `python3 tools/check_layouts.py`: every view id the code uses exists in its layouts.
- `python3 tools/test_migration.py`: upgrades a build-6 (schema v1) and a Phase 1 (schema v2) database to v3 with the SQL in `core/Schema.kt` and checks nothing is lost.
- `python3 tools/test_store_sql.py`: runs the Store's own SQL (`core/StoreSql.kt`) for deletes, sticky context, import dedupe and rollback, the dirty-day counters, calendar export records and the vault cache.
- `tools/run_core_tests.sh`: the unit tests for `core/` (pure Kotlin) without the Android SDK; CI runs them with `gradle :app:testDebugUnitTest`.
- `python3 tools/daylio_decode.py --summary file.daylio`: decode a Daylio backup. Never commit a real backup; the test fixtures are synthetic (`tools/make_daylio_fixtures.py`).
